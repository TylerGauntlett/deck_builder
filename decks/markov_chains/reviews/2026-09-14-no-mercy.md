# No Mercy — markov_chains

**Date:** 2026-09-14
**Deck:** markov_chains — Edgar Markov, Commander, bracket 3, $40/card cap
**Verdict: NO**

One line: the deck already turns creature deaths into drain *on its own terms*,
with 7 sacrifice outlets and a land that does No Mercy's job better, so No Mercy
is paying 4 mana and a card for an effect the opponent decides when to grant me,
after I've taken the damage.

| Axis | No Mercy |
|---|---|
| Whose permanents | **Any** creature — mine included (per the ruling) |
| Whose turn | Any turn |

## 3. The steel-man — and it is better than it looks

I want to make this case properly, because No Mercy is *not* just a pillowfort card
in this deck, and rejecting it as "defensive card in an aggro deck" would be lazy.

Counting the 99's death payoffs **grouped by trigger scope** (verified oracle text,
not the card names):

**Fires on ANY creature dying — including opponents' (7):**

| Card | Clause |
|---|---|
| Blood Artist | "Whenever this creature or **another creature** dies, target player loses 1 life and you gain 1 life." |
| Cordial Vampire | "Whenever this creature or **another creature** dies, put a +1/+1 counter on **each Vampire you control**." |
| Vein Ripper | "Whenever **a creature** dies, target opponent loses 2 life and you gain 2 life." |
| Elenda, the Dusk Rose | "Whenever **another creature** dies, put a +1/+1 counter on Elenda." |
| Blade of the Bloodchief | "Whenever **a creature** dies, put a +1/+1 counter on equipped creature. If equipped creature is a Vampire, put two instead." |
| High-Society Hunter | "Whenever another **nontoken** creature dies, draw a card." |
| Patron of the Vein | "Whenever a creature **an opponent controls** dies, exile it and put a +1/+1 counter on each Vampire you control." |

**Fires ONLY on my creatures dying — No Mercy does nothing for these (4):**
Cruel Celebrant ("another creature or planeswalker **you control**"), Zulaport
Cutthroat ("another creature **you control**"), Bastion of Remembrance ("a creature
**you control**"), Grave Pact ("a creature **you control**").

So the honest steel-man is: an opponent's attacker dying to No Mercy drains for 1
(Blood Artist) + 2 (Vein Ripper), pumps the whole Vampire board (Cordial Vampire,
Patron of the Vein), grows Elenda and the equipped creature, and draws a card
(High-Society Hunter). That is **seven** payoffs off a card the opponent walked
into. It is a repeatable, untargeted, ward-proof removal engine that feeds the
aristocrat half of the deck. That's a real argument and I'm not dismissing it.

## 4. Why it still fails

**a) The deck already manufactures deaths, on demand, at instant speed, on its own
terms.** Verified sacrifice outlets in the 99: Carrion Feeder ({0}), Viscera Seer
({0}), Bloodthrone Vampire ({0}), Indulgent Aristocrat ({2}), Phyrexian Tower
({0}, land), Master of Dark Rites ({T}, adds {B}{B}{B}), Village Rites, plus Baron
Bertram Graywater's {1}{B} draw outlet and High-Society Hunter's attack trigger.
**Seven free-or-cheap outlets.** Edgar's eminence makes a 1/1 Vampire every time I
cast a Vampire, so the fuel is free too. The deck's problem has never been *finding
deaths* — it's a deck that can fire those seven payoffs whenever it wants. No Mercy
adds deaths I don't control, at a time I don't choose, in exchange for life.

**b) Vault of the Archangel is already in the deck and is strictly better at this
job.** `{2}{W}{B}, {T}: Creatures you control gain deathtouch and lifelink until
end of turn.` Same outcome — attackers die, all 7 payoffs fire — except: I **gain**
life instead of losing it, it's on a **land** so it costs no spell slot, and it
also makes my *attacks* lethal-on-contact drains. No Mercy only works on defense.
The deck additionally has Vampire of the Dire Moon and Qarsi Revenant as standing
deathtouch blockers, and Sorin, Imperious Bloodlord's +1 grants deathtouch+lifelink.
The "punish attackers" role is filled four times over, by cards that also attack.

Vault's honest edge-case loss to No Mercy: Vault needs {2}{W}{B} open and untapped
blockers; No Mercy works while I'm tapped out. That's real, and it is not worth 4
mana and a card.

**c) The trigger condition is opponent-controlled and self-harming.** "Whenever a
creature deals damage to you" means I have already taken the damage, and the
opponent decides whether to grant the trigger at all. With three opponents and 38
creatures on my side, the rational play is simply to attack someone else — at which
point No Mercy is a blank that cost me the most important turn-4 in the deck. And
in the case where it matters most — I'm low, someone swings lethal — destroying the
creature after damage resolves is too late.

**d) The four-drop slot is the deck's contested slot.** 21 nonland cards at MV 4+,
and turn 4 is when the deck deploys a lord or an engine: Sanctum Seeker, Bloodline
Keeper, Vampire Nocturnus, Bloodletter of Aclazotz, Elenda, Baron Bertram, Edgar
Charmed Groom, Mirkwood Bats, Grave Pact. No Mercy is the only card in that bracket
that affects nothing when it lands.

**e) It doesn't answer what actually kills this deck.** Not board wipes, not
targeted removal on Edgar, not combo, not noncreature damage. It answers creature
combat — the one axis where a 38-creature board with four deathtouch effects is
already strongest.

## 8. What would change this

One thing, and it's a genuine axis I can't verify from the repo:

**If this deck is losing games specifically to creature combat damage** — repeatedly
alpha-struck as archenemy and dying with a board of blockers you couldn't profitably
use — then No Mercy is measuring on a dimension I've assumed away, and I'd re-run it.
Two questions settle it:

1. How long do games in this pod actually run, and how often are you the archenemy?
2. When you lose, what kills you — combat damage, a wipe you couldn't rebuild from,
   or a combo?

If the answer to (2) is "combat damage, often, from multiple players at once," come
back and I'll re-run this on that axis. If it's wipes or combo — which is what a
38-creature aristocrats deck usually dies to — No Mercy answers neither, and the
verdict stands as written.

If the gap you were feeling is "I need to not die while I set up," note the deck
already carries Teferi's Protection, Clever Concealment, Akroma's Will and Vault of
the Archangel. That role is not thin.
