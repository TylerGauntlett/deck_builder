## Willowrush Verge — **ADD, cut Botanical Sanctum**

> {T}: Add {U}. {T}: Add {G}. Activate only if you control a Forest or an Island.

- **Best case:** always enters untapped, and taps for both colours whenever you
  control one of 26 basics (14 Forest, 12 Island).
- **Against Botanical Sanctum** ("enters tapped unless you control two or fewer other
  lands"): Sanctum is tapped from your 4th land on. A 44-land deck with Aesi, Azusa,
  Dryad, Burgeoning, Exploration and Oracle of Mul Daya reaches its 4th land around
  turn 3, so for most of a turn 9–12 game Sanctum is a tapland. Verge's only loss is a
  turn-1 Verge with no basic out, which makes U only.
- Land for land, no role or count changes. Low impact — the audit found the mana base
  is not a weakness — but the swap costs nothing.
- **Runner-up cut:** Dreamroot Cascade (tapped only on your first two land drops, so it
  is closer to Verge than Sanctum is).
- Price: $4.95 (fetched 2026-10-23 10:51 UTC). Owned.

## Nissa, Worldsoul Speaker — **ADD IF you don't take the audit's Aetherize-for-Ghalta swap; then cut Ghalta**

> Landfall — Whenever a land you control enters, you get {E}{E}.
> You may pay eight {E} rather than pay the mana cost for permanent spells you cast.

- **Best case:** the only card in the deck that turns landfall into casting spells
  (0 other energy or free-cast effects). Every land is a quarter of a free permanent;
  fetches count twice (5 sacrifice-fetch lands: Evolving Wilds, Terramorphic Expanse,
  Fabled Passage, Myriad Landscape, Blighted Woodland), and Ancient Greenwarden makes
  the trigger fire an extra time. Twelve permanents at MV 6+ to cast this way (Aesi,
  Greenwarden, Kodama of the East Tree, Rampaging Baloths, Avenger, Cultivator
  Colossus, Jin-Gitaxias, Koma, Nezahal, Craterhoof, Terastodon, Ghalta). Energy
  carries over between turns (ruling 2025-02-07). Fills the MV4 hole (2 → 3). Matches
  the deck's landfall preference.
- **Rate vs Lotus Cobra:** Cobra gives 1 mana per land, used straight away. Nissa gives
  1/4 of a permanent per land; for an MV8 spell that's worth 2 mana per land, and for
  anything MV4 or under it's worse than Cobra. So her value comes from the top end.
- **The attack — marginal impact:** she builds energy fastest exactly when Aesi and
  the extra land drops are already online, which is when the deck is already ahead.
  With 1 land a turn (Aesi dead, slow draw), she makes a free spell every four turns.
  She does nothing against any of the pod's four threats (combo, go-wide, mill,
  aristocrats). She's a 3/3 for 4, and any energy you've banked is useless once she
  dies (no other energy payoffs).
- **Disagreement check:** 1.2% of Aesi decks (194/16,129). The community mostly passes
  on her, and the marginal-impact problem above is a good enough reason. Nothing makes
  this deck special enough to override that.
- **Why ADD IF and not NO:** the cut has to be worse, and Ghalta is. The audit already
  called it the third finisher behind Craterhoof and Overwhelming Stampede (same
  reasoning here). Nissa does something nothing else in the deck does; Ghalta does what
  two other cards do. But the audit gave Ghalta's slot to **Aetherize**, the deck's only
  mass answer, and that addresses how the deck actually loses. Interaction beats engine
  for that slot.
- Not Goldvein Hydra: the audit gave that slot to Reality Shift (exile vs aristocrats),
  for the same reason.
- Price: $0.20 (fetched 2026-10-23 10:51 UTC). Owned.

## Sab-Sunen, Luxa Embodied — **NO**

> At the beginning of your first main phase, put a +1/+1 counter on Sab-Sunen. Then if
> it has an odd number of counters on it, draw two cards.
> Sab-Sunen can't attack or block unless it has an even number of counters on it.

- **Best case:** an indestructible draw engine that doesn't need lands, so it survives
  destroy-wipes that Aesi and Tatyova don't, and keeps drawing when land drops stall.
- **Rate:** 2 cards every other turn, starting the turn after you cast it, so about 1
  card a turn for 5 mana. Tatyova (same MV, same colours) draws 1 per land, and this
  deck plays 2+ lands a turn. It can only block on the round after an even-counter
  turn, so it's an unreliable wall against go-wide.
- **Redundancy:** the 5th repeatable draw engine in a deck whose commander is a draw
  engine. The audit also flagged that the deck can **deck itself** (Aesi + 6 extra
  land-drop effects + a mill player). More draw makes that worse.
- Survives being free: yes. Price isn't the reason.
- Price: $1.96 (fetched 2026-10-23 10:51 UTC). Owned.

  makes better use of it.
- Survives being free: yes.
- Price: $0.91 (fetched 2026-10-23 10:51 UTC). Owned.

## Addendum — Ghalta vs Jin-Gitaxias as Nissa's cut (user challenge)

Re-fetched 2026-10-23 12:38 UTC. The user's point is that Ghalta works with the other finishers.

- **Overwhelming Stampede: true.** "+X/+X, where X is the greatest power among
  creatures you control" → Ghalta makes it +12 to the whole team. The next-biggest
  bodies are Terastodon 9 and Nezahal 7. Arixmethes is also 12, but only after its five
  slumber counters are gone. This weakens the "redundant third finisher" reason I gave
  for cutting Ghalta.
- **Finale of Devastation: mostly no.** Fetching Ghalta needs X=12 (14 mana), and
  X ≥ 10 already gives the whole team +X/+X and haste. Fetching Craterhoof at X=8 is
  cheaper. Finale's haste does let Ghalta attack the turn it enters, but that applies to
  any creature.
- **Jin-Gitaxias's case, which neither review weighed:** "Whenever an opponent casts an
  artifact, instant, or sorcery spell, counter that spell" (once per turn). That
  directly answers the pod's #1 threat, spell-based combo. It also copies one of your
  21 artifacts, instants and sorceries each turn, including Finale (a second tutor and
  pump) and Cyclonic Rift. The audit cut it for having "zero land synergy" and never
  weighed this clause, so that cut was under-argued.
- **For Jin-Gitaxias as the cut:** it is one of 7 cards at MV7 (Avenger, Cultivator
  Colossus, Jin-Gitaxias, Koma, Nezahal, Turntimber Symbiosis, Verdant Kraken), and
  swapping MV7 for MV4 helps the curve more than cutting Ghalta, which is cheap once
  the board is wide.

**Verdict holds: cut Ghalta, Jin-Gitaxias is runner-up.** The Stampede pairing is real,
but it's one card, and it improves a game that's already being won. Jin-Gitaxias's
counter clause is the only card in the deck that answers spell combo on every turn it
stays on the battlefield. **What would flip it:** if the pod's combo kills are
mostly creature-based (the clause does nothing to those), cut Jin-Gitaxias instead.

## Addendum 2 — Nezahal as Nissa's cut (user proposal)

Fetched 2026-10-23 14:56 UTC. EDHREC: Nezahal is in 34.0% of Aesi decks (5,922/17,404).

> You have no maximum hand size. Whenever an opponent casts a noncreature spell, draw a
> card. Discard three cards: Exile Nezahal. Return it ... at the beginning of the next end step.

- **Card draw:** this deck has 4 repeatable draw engines (Aesi, Tatyova, Rhystic Study,
  Nezahal). Nezahal is the 4th. Its trigger ("an opponent casts a noncreature spell")
  overlaps Rhystic Study's ("an opponent casts a spell").
- **No maximum hand size:** Nezahal is 1 of 3 cards with this, alongside Reliquary
  Tower and Thought Vessel.
- **Curve:** Nezahal is one of 7 cards at MV7, so cutting it gives the same 7 → 4 curve
  relief as cutting Jin-Gitaxias. Unlike that cut, it leaves Jin-Gitaxias's anti-spell
  clause in the deck, and unlike cutting Ghalta, it keeps the Stampede +12 turn.
- **Library:** the audit flagged that the deck can deck itself. Fewer draw engines
  helps there.
- **What you lose:** the most resilient threat in the deck. It can't be countered, and
  with Aesi filling your hand, discarding three to dodge removal or a wipe is cheap. It
  is also your only land-independent draw besides Rhystic Study if Aesi is removed.
- **Disagreement check:** 34% of Aesi decks run it, so the community likes it. What's
  different here is that this list already has Rhystic Study, two other
  no-max-hand-size cards, and a mill opponent pressuring the library. That's a real
  difference, but not a huge one.

**Revised:** Nezahal is the best cut for Nissa, then Ghalta, then Jin-Gitaxias. **What
would flip it:** if Aesi gets removed often in your games, Nezahal is the backup draw
engine and should stay. In that case go back to cutting Ghalta.

Consistency note: Sab-Sunen was rejected partly as a "5th draw engine." With Nezahal
gone it would be the 4th, but it still loses to Tatyova on rate and adds to the library
risk, so it stays NO and doesn't take this slot.

## Addendum 3 — Terastodon as Nissa's cut (user proposal)

Fetched 2026-10-23 14:59 UTC. EDHREC: Terastodon is in 14.6% of Aesi decks (2,539/17,404).

> When this creature enters, you may destroy up to three target noncreature permanents.
> For each permanent put into a graveyard this way, its controller creates a 3/3 green Elephant creature token. [9/9, MV8]

It does three jobs:

1. **Interaction.** It is the only card in the deck that answers *more than one*
   noncreature permanent. Every other answer takes one target: Acidic Slime ("target
   artifact, enchantment, or land"), Reclamation Sage ("target artifact or
   enchantment"), Beast Within ("target permanent") and Cyclonic Rift unoverloaded.
   Against artifact or enchantment combo it can clear the whole setup.
2. **It turns lands into a board.** Destroy three of your own lands for three 3/3s
   (9 power for Ghalta's cost or a Stampede turn). The lands go to the graveyard,
   where Ramunap Excavator and Ancient Greenwarden ("You may play lands from your
   graveyard") let you replay them for more landfall, and Greenwarden makes each
   landfall trigger fire twice.
3. **Finale target** at X=8, the same cost as Craterhoof.

**Against Nezahal:** Nezahal is the 4th draw engine and the 3rd no-max-hand-size card.
Terastodon is the deck's only multi-target noncreature answer. Cutting the redundant
card beats cutting the unique one, and it matches the audit's finding that interaction
is what the deck is short on. The one thing in the cut's favour is the curve: it's one
of 2 cards at MV8.

**Terastodon stays.** Final order for Nissa's slot: **Nezahal, then Ghalta, then
Jin-Gitaxias. Terastodon is not a candidate.**

## Addendum 4 — Nezahal stays (user challenge, verdict reversed)

The user reports Ghalta has been removed. Their argument: card draw is how this deck
finds its game-enders, because it has so few tutors.

- **Tutors:** the deck has 1 creature tutor, Finale of Devastation, which finds
  Craterhoof at X=8 or wins outright at X≥10. Turntimber Symbiosis digs 7 cards deep
  for a creature. Without Ghalta, the team-wide game-enders are **Craterhoof,
  Overwhelming Stampede and Finale (X≥10)**. Card draw is the main way to find any of
  them.
- **Nezahal's rate was misjudged.** I called it "the 4th draw engine" and stopped
  there, which is failure mode 2 (comparing effect, not rate). Nezahal's trigger has
  no "unless that player pays {1}" escape (Rhystic Study has one) and no land
  requirement (Aesi and Tatyova need land drops). Unverified estimate: in a 4-player
  pod facing a spell-combo player, it draws several cards per round. After Aesi, it is
  likely the highest-volume engine in the deck, and the only one that keeps going when
  your land drops stall.
- The library risk from the audit still applies, but the audit's own advice was not to
  pre-empt it.

**Revised:** Nezahal is **not** a cut. With Ghalta already gone, Nissa's slot is
filled, so the remaining cut ranking (Jin-Gitaxias, Terastodon) is moot.
