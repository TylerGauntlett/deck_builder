# angels — shape review (2026-10-02)

Deck state reviewed: `complete.txt` (100 cards, the build after the build-out, ceiling, proliferate, budget and mass-token reviews).
Prices: Scryfall cheapest printing, fetched 2026-10-02 15:44 UTC. EDHREC: Giada commander page, 36,013 decks.
Constraints (meta.json): bracket 3, ≤3 Game Changers, no early or tutor-dependent infinites, $40/card, ~$300 total (~$255 spent, ~$45 left).

## Keystone
- **Primary action:** put Angels onto the battlefield. That means 24 Angels including Giada, plus Angel-token makers (Court of Grace, Serra the Benevolent, Resplendent Angel, Valkyrie Harbinger, Ob Nixilis). Giada's mana and four cost reducers make it cheap.
- **Capitalization:** each Angel entering becomes **board-wide growth**, through two parallel chains:
  - *Enter → counters:* Giada ("enters with an additional +1/+1 counter … for each Angel you already control"), Cathars' Crusade, Metastatic Evangel (proliferate on nontoken entering).
  - *Enter → lifegain → counters:* Bishop of Wings (4 per Angel), Righteous Valkyrie (toughness per Angel/Cleric), Dazzling Angel, Seraph Sanctuary. These feed Archangel of Thune (counter on **each creature**), Lyra, Archangel of Dawn (counter on each Angel), Exemplar of Light and Dawn of Hope (draw).
- **Conversion to a win:** an alpha strike in the air, which is hard to block in a pod that wins on the ground. The finishers are Akroma's Will (flying + double strike, plus protection with Giada out), Elesh Norn, Sephara and Avacyn. Ob Nixilis and Elesh Norn are the overwhelming-interaction layer, with one-sided wipes.
- **Nonbos found:**
  - *None structural.* `combos.py` with all adds: 0 combos.
  - **The win layer does not use the lifegain half of the capitalization.** The deck routinely sits at 50–70 life, but every finisher converts *power*, not *life*. Life only becomes counters, which is the same output as the other chain. That gap is the main finding below.
  - **Commander dependence is high and has one guard.** Flawless Maneuver ("If you control a commander, you may cast this spell without paying its mana cost"), Akroma's Will ("If you control a commander … choose both"), Angelic Field Marshal (Lieutenant), Folk Hero, Norn's Choirmaster and Tome of Legends all key off Giada, a 2/2. Only Swiftfoot Boots protects her before turn 6–7 (Sephara and Avacyn come later).
  - *Minor:* Magus of the Moat ("Creatures without flying can't attack") also stops your own Elesh Norn, Samurai, Servos and Soldiers from attacking. Acceptable, since they are anthem and counter fodder, and Akroma's Will gives flying for the kill turn.

## Commander role → target shape
Giada is an **amplifier** (each Angel enters bigger) and a **mana source** (Angel-only W). The commander covers part of the amplifier layer and part of mana, so the target is an **inverted T**: a broad Angel generator base, a thinner amplifier layer weighted to *compounding* engines rather than static anthems, and a small payoff cap.

Plan adjustments:
- The deck is loud. It takes the monarch and builds a big board of fliers, so it needs **protection** over suppression, and specifically protection for Giada.
- Suppression is already heavy: 15 cards plus Eiganjo and three pillowfort pieces. **Hold.**
- Mana: 37 + a cycling land, 9 rocks or reducers, and Giada. **Over-supplied** at an average nonland MV of about 3.5.

## Goldfish checklist
1. **Giada alive on turn 4?** Count the games where Flawless Maneuver couldn't be cast for free or Akroma's Will was single-mode. With Giver and Boots this should be rare.
2. **Life at end of turn 6–8.** If you're routinely at 55+, Angel of Destiny is a live win and the plan has two axes. If not, the lifegain chain is thinner than it looks.
3. **Flooding?** With 37 lands, Angel of the Ruins' plainscycling and Secluded Steppe's cycling, note hands with 3+ lands and nothing to do on turn 5+. If it still floods, cut Radiant Fountain next.
4. **Which turn presents lethal or the alt-win?** Expect turn 7–8 at bracket 3. Then add 1–2 turns for real-table friction: attacks split across three opponents, and pillowfort tax on you.
5. **After a wipe:** how fast does the board come back? Court of Grace, Emeria recursion and Emeria's Call should rebuild within 2 turns.

## Follow-up: Bruna, the Fading Light alongside Gisela (2026-10-02) — NO
- **Meld is a weak reason.** Gisela's trigger ("exile them, then meld them into Brisela") isn't a "may", so the meld is forced. It needs both cards to survive to your end step, and turns two bodies into **one** removal target. You lose every counter on both and one flier from the Sephara and Akroma's Will count. Brisela, Voice of Nightmares (9/10 flying, first strike, vigilance, lifelink; "Your opponents can't cast spells with mana value 3 or less") is strong, but it is a two-specific-card line in a deck with no tutors.
- **On its own merits, Bruna is close.** It's an Angel, so the discounts apply, and its cast trigger ("return target Angel or Human creature card from your graveyard to the battlefield") gives two Angels entering at once. But the top end is already 6 cards at mana value 7–8 (Sephara, Angel of the Ruins, Elesh Norn, Ob Nixilis, Emeria's Call, Avacyn). Recursion is covered by Emeria, the Sky Ruin, and the build-out review already said NO for this reason.
- **Gisela stays** as a 4-mana lifelink first-striker that feeds Thune and Lyra AoD each combat. It was never chosen for the meld.
- Revisit if goldfishing shows games regularly going long *and* Angels piling up in the graveyard. Then Bruna for Angel of the Ruins is the like-for-like swap (cheapest print $4.02, fetched 2026-10-02 16:14 UTC; 57.8% of Giada decks on EDHREC).
